Open Source Plumbing The Physical Problem

Why Normal Tech Doesn't Work in Orbit

You can't open a browser, swipe a card, or run regular crypto over a 5-minute radio pass.

What Breaks on the Wire
  • No Persistent Internet: Orbital passes last roughly 5 to 8 minutes over ~1 kbps links. There is no continuous connection to hold open a socket or query a balance.
  • The Wire Format Problem: Everyday Web3 wallets, JSON-RPC calls (300 B – 1.5 KB), and even Protobuf schemas (112 B – 725 B) carry too much baggage for a single burst—quickly blowing past a LoRa radio's 255-byte frame ceiling and forcing messy packet fragmentation.
  • Flight Computers Can't Crash: Running heavy language runtimes on bare-metal hardware introduces heap fragmentation and unrecoverable freeze-ups[cite: 1].
  • No Two-Way Handshakes: By the time a standard multi-round payment handshake negotiates, the satellite has already passed over the horizon[cite: 1].
What This Actually Enables
  • Autonomous Refueling: A craft docks at an orbital propellant depot, settles the fuel bill automatically, and undocks without human intervention.
  • Buying Relay Bandwidth: Sat A has data to downlink but no ground pass; Sat B has line-of-sight. Sat A pays Sat B micro-cents to relay the packets down[cite: 1].
  • Offloading Edge Compute: Remote sensors and small satellites can sell telemetry or buy compute from adjacent nodes on the fly[cite: 1].
  • A 192-Byte Receipt: Strip away all web bloat so an entire signed payment intent transmits in a single sub-second radio burst[cite: 1].

"Think of a Tesla pulling up to a charger and paying automatically without you ever touching an app. That's what's missing in space. When private stations, fuel depots, and satellite swarms go up, machines will need to buy fuel, compute, or bandwidth off each other. There's no human up there tapping a card, and regular internet doesn't work. Void is just the card reader for machines: they shake hands over radio, settle in digital dollars, and get on with their job."

Binary Architecture Rule of 8 Alignment Zero Copy

Wire Specification: Dual-Tier Systems Framing

A unified memory layout that bridges institutional space bands (CCSDS — Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems) with community SDRs and TinyGS listening stations (SNLP — Space Network Layer Protocol).

Packet Type Operational Role Enterprise Tier (CCSDS) Community Tier (SNLP) Cryptographic Enforcement
Packet A Service Invoice (Seller) 72 Bytes (6B Hdr + 66B Body) 80 Bytes (14B Hdr + 66B Body) Unsigned cleartext; CRC32 validated
Packet B Signed Payment Intent 184 Bytes 192 Bytes Ed25519 signature + deterministic ChaCha20
Packet ACK Unlock Command & Relay 120 Bytes (embeds 88B encrypted unlock for Sat A) 136 Bytes (embeds 96B encrypted unlock for Sat A) CRC32 pre-gate + anti-collision tag (0xAC)
Packet C / D Execution Receipt & Downlink 104B (C) / 128B (D) 104B (C) / 136B (D) Sat A PUF signature; Magic (0xD0)
Packet H / Beat Handshake & System Health 112 Bytes (Packet H) 34-Byte Body (Heartbeat) X25519 ECDH Ephemeral Key Exchange
1. Mod-8 Header Congruence

The 14-byte SNLP header matches the 6-byte CCSDS header modulo 8 (14 ≡ 6 mod 8). Both tiers share the exact same body struct with zero runtime branching and native 64-bit alignment.

2. Collision Proofing (Hamming 4)

Packet ACK (0xAC) and Delivery D (0xD0) share identical 122-byte body lengths. A 1-byte magic discriminant with Hamming distance 4 ensures no single-bit RF error can cause misrouting.

3. Zero-Wire Deterministic Nonce

ChaCha20 nonces are derived as sat_id || epoch_ts. Eliminates 12 bytes of wire bloat, removes RNG latency, and makes nonce reuse mathematically impossible over monotonic clocks.

Presenter Perspective
"The reason you see two tiers here is simple: CCSDS connects to licensed commercial satellites, while SNLP adds a software sync word so open-source SDRs, universities, and TinyGS antennas can easily lock onto our packets in noisy LoRa bands. Under the hood, they share the exact same payload structs—the 14-byte SNLP header and 6-byte CCSDS header align on 8-byte boundaries, so our C++ firmware parses both with zero memory copies[cite: 2, 3, 8]. When ground settles an invoice, it returns Packet ACK carrying an encrypted 88-byte unlock parcel that Sat B blindly relays to Sat A. And to make sure cosmic rays or radio noise can't scramble packet types, we use distinct magic bytes so a single bit-flip can never mistake a delivery receipt for an unlock command[cite: 5, 6]."

First Principles Zero Compromise Design Rationale

Why Build It This Way?

Every strict constraint in Void Protocol exists because the laws of physics and radio don't care about standard web conventions.

The Common Assumption The Orbital Reality The Architectural Choice
"Just use Protobuf or JSON-RPC" Schemas with dynamic fields balloon to 300 B–1.5 KB. Over LoRa, that forces multi-packet fragmentation where a single dropped frame kills the payment. Fixed 192 B Frame: An entire signed intent fits inside one atomic radio burst with 63 bytes of headroom below the 255 B PHY ceiling[cite: 1, 3].
"Why C++14 instead of Rust, Go, or Python?" GC runtimes suit non-critical payloads, not flight comms. While NASA targets Rust, battle-tested radio drivers and aerospace toolchains remain anchored in C/C++. Zero-Heap C++14: Interfaces directly with proven radio drivers today under strict SEI CERT static allocation, leaving a clean path to Rust as embedded crates mature.
"Just run a two-way handshake (TLS)" LEO passes last 5–8 minutes over intermittent ~1 kbps links[cite: 1]. Multi-roundtrip negotiation means the bird passes over the horizon before the socket even authenticates[cite: 1]. Store-and-Forward Asynchrony: One-shot ephemeral handshakes (AOS) and deterministic nonces that settle the critical path in under 60 seconds[cite: 1].
"Why not build separate protocols for space and IoT?" Divergent codebases double verification costs. On flight computers, wasted CPU cycles copying data directly drain battery power. Unified 32/64-Bit Alignment: Both tiers share identical 8-byte aligned structs. Frames process with zero memory copies, minimizing CPU cycles and power draw[cite: 1].
Presenter Perspective
"To wrap up, people often ask: 'Why so much rigor? Why obsess over 8-byte alignment, zero heap allocations, and custom packet framing?' The answer is simple: space doesn't tolerate web assumptions. If your runtime runs out of heap, your flight computer dies. If your transaction needs three round-trips or balloons past 255 bytes, the satellite flies over the horizon before the money moves. We didn't choose these constraints to be difficult—we engineered Void Protocol to survive the physical realities of the wire."
